Section 13Fairness: a Phase 1 promise, and the result it produced

Phase 1 wrote that quantifying demographic disparities before modelling is "what makes a later fairness check interpretable rather than merely alarming". That sentence created an obligation, and this section discharges it. It also produces the most uncomfortable number in the project: removing every protected attribute from the model removes only of the demographic disparity in its decisions.

13.1 Why the Phase 1 baseline is what makes this interpretable

A fairness audit that reports "the model flags men more often than women" is uninformative on its own, because men in this dataset default more often than women. Without the baseline you cannot distinguish three completely different situations:

A
The model creates a disparity. Groups default at equal rates, the model treats them differently. Unambiguously a model defect.
B
The model preserves a disparity. It reproduces the difference already present in the outcomes. A faithful model of an unequal world — which is a policy question, not a bug.
C
The model amplifies a disparity. A real gap exists, and the model's decisions exaggerate it. This needs its own argument, because the amplification is the model's contribution and not the data's.

Distinguishing these requires the observed rates, measured before modelling. Phase 1 reported them, so the comparison below is available:

So the honest sentence is: "the model amplifies a disparity that already exists in the data rather than creating one." Both halves matter. The first half is the model's responsibility. The second half is why "the model is biased" is too crude a description — a model fitted to these outcomes could not produce equal selection rates without being deliberately constrained to, and that constraint would be a policy decision with its own costs.

13.2 What is measured, and where

Four attributes — SEX, EDUCATION, MARRIAGE and a derived AGE_BAND (<30, 30–39, 40–49, 50+) — each with per-subgroup ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, recall, FPR, precision, selection rate and a Wilson interval on the observed rate. Two models: the full 23-feature model and the -feature no-demographics model from §10.3's ablation.

Three design decisions in this experiment
  1. Evaluated out-of-fold on the training portion, not on the test set. Nothing is selected here, so it needs no test-set licence (§5.6) — and OOF gives subgroup samples about four times larger than the hold-out would. For EDUCATION category 3 and the MARRIAGE tail that is the difference between a usable Wilson interval and a meaningless one. The frozen τ = from §9 is applied unchanged.
  2. wilson() and rate_table() are the Phase 1 functions, ported unmodified into src/metrics.py. The intervals are computed the same way as the proposal's, so the two phases' numbers are directly comparable rather than approximately so.
  3. Several criteria are reported, because they conflict. There is no single "fairness" number, and choosing one silently would be the methodological error.

The impossibility result — worth naming explicitly

Chouldechova (2017) and Kleinberg et al. (2017) proved that when base rates differ between groups, calibration within groups and equalised odds cannot both hold except in degenerate cases. Base rates here differ by on SEX and on EDUCATION, so this is not a hypothetical: no amount of tuning makes this model satisfy both criteria. Reporting several metrics and naming the trade-off is therefore the only defensible option — reporting one and calling the model "fair" would be claiming something mathematically unavailable.

13.4 The proxy test: does removing the columns remove the influence?

The intuitive remedy is to delete the protected attributes. §10.3's ablation already produced the model, so the test costs nothing extra: refit on the financial features only, apply the frozen threshold, re-measure every gap.

The headline: of the disparity survives

Removing SEX, EDUCATION, MARRIAGE and AGE costs ROC-AUC — nothing, well inside one fold-SD, consistent with §10.3's finding that the demographic block is nearly free to drop. And yet the SEX selection-rate gap falls only from to : of it remains in a model that cannot see sex at all.

The mechanism is proxying. LIMIT_BAL, bill amounts and payment amounts are correlated with sex, education and age in this population — credit limits in particular, since they encode the bank's own earlier assessment (§11.6). The model reconstructs enough of the protected attribute from the financial columns to reproduce most of the disparate impact.

So fairness-through-unawareness does not work here — it hides the influence rather than removing it, exactly as Barocas & Selbst (2016) predict and as the Phase 1 proposal already cited. Worse than ineffective, it is actively harmful to governance: the disparity is still there, but the attribute needed to detect it has been deleted from the pipeline. You cannot audit what you refuse to record.

Why this is a strong result for the Summary

It is counter-intuitive, it is quantified, it is predicted by a paper the proposal already cited, and it only became measurable because §10.3's group ablation had already produced the no-demographics arm — the plan noted this was "one line of extra work" and it yielded the most interesting fairness finding in the project. It also reverses a plausible recommendation: the naive conclusion from the AUC cost would be "drop the demographics, they're free". The correct conclusion is to keep measuring them precisely because dropping them changes the decisions far less than it changes your ability to notice.

13.5 What this section does not claim

Scope, stated before someone else states it for you
  • No mitigation was applied. No reweighting, no per-group thresholds, no adversarial debiasing, no fairness-constrained objective. This is a measurement, which is what Phase 1 promised. Per-group thresholds would reduce the parity gap mechanically, but setting a different τ by sex is itself disparate treatment and is unlawful in most consumer-credit regimes — so it is not a neutral technical fix and would need a legal argument this project is not positioned to make.
  • "Amplifies" is not "causes". The disparity originates in the data. The amplification factor (× on SEX) is the model's contribution, and that is the number this project is answerable for.
  • The protected attributes are coarse. SEX is binary, EDUCATION is a four-level proxy for socioeconomic status, and the dataset is Taiwan 2005. Nothing here transfers to another population without re-measurement.
  • Intersectional gaps are not examined. Each attribute is analysed marginally. Subgroup sizes do not support a 2 × 4 × 3 × 4 cross-tabulation — the Wilson intervals would be wider than the effects — and claiming an intersectional result on a dozen clients per cell would be the kind of over-claiming §4.9 exists to prevent.

13.6 The code

experiments/exp13_fairness.pyexcerpt
# The frozen OOF threshold from exp10. NOT re-optimised per group, and not
# re-derived here -- per-group thresholds would be disparate treatment, and
# re-deriving would risk a different number than the one the report quotes.
tau = float(json.loads(
    (ARTIFACTS / "threshold_r6.json").read_text()
)["tau"])

for attr, labels in PROTECTED.items():
    for code, name in labels.items():
        m = X[attr] == code
        p, y_g = oof_proba[m], y[m]
        flag = p >= tau                      # frozen scalar, same for every group
        tn, fp, fn, tp = confusion_matrix(y_g, flag).ravel()
        lo, hi = wilson(y_g.sum(), len(y_g))   # Phase 1 function, unmodified
        rows.append({
            "group": name, "n": int(m.sum()),
            "observed_default_rate_pct": 100 * y_g.mean(),
            "wilson_lo_pct": 100 * lo, "wilson_hi_pct": 100 * hi,
            "roc_auc": roc_auc_score(y_g, p),
            "recall": tp / (tp + fn), "fpr": fp / (fp + tn),
            "selection_rate": flag.mean(),
            "mean_predicted": p.mean(),   # per-group calibration check
        })
Two things that excerpt gets right
  • τ is read from an artefact, not recomputed. If this script re-derived the cost-optimal threshold it could land on a slightly different value than §9 reported, and the fairness numbers would then describe an operating point that appears nowhere else in the report. Reading the frozen scalar makes the sections reconcile by construction.
  • mean_predicted is captured per group. That is the within-group calibration check, and it is the quantity the Chouldechova impossibility result is about. Collecting it is what allows the trade-off to be demonstrated in the widget rather than merely cited from a paper.
Self-check: if the model amplifies disparity, why ship it?

That is the right question, and it is a governance question rather than a modelling one — which is itself the honest answer. What this section establishes is the factual basis any such decision would need: the disparity exists in the outcomes ( on SEX), the model amplifies it ×, and the obvious remedy of deleting the protected columns recovers only of the gap while leaving intact.

What a deploying institution would need beyond this: a legal review against local consumer-credit law, a decision on which fairness criterion is the governing one (they conflict, §13.2), and monitoring of the realised selection rates in production. The appropriate claim for an academic submission is that the disparity has been measured and reported rather than discovered after deployment — and Phase 1's commitment to quantify the baseline first is what makes the measurement interpretable instead of merely alarming.

Self-check: why out-of-fold rather than on the test set? Isn't the test set the "real" evaluation?

For performance, yes — and §14 does exactly that. For subgroup analysis the hold-out is the wrong instrument for two reasons.

Sample size. The test set has rows, so EDUCATION level 3 and the MARRIAGE tail would be a few hundred clients each, with Wilson intervals wide enough to swamp every gap being discussed. Out-of-fold gives rows — roughly four times as many per subgroup — and every one of them is still a prediction made by a model that had not seen that row.

Test-set discipline. §5.6 licenses exactly one experiment to touch the hold-out. Fairness selects nothing: the model is frozen, the threshold is frozen, the subgroups are pre-specified. Spending test-set exposure on an analysis that does not need it would weaken the one number that does — the final reported ROC-AUC — for no gain in precision.
