Section 1Orientation: what you built and why it hangs together

This guide explains the entire Phase 2 submission from first principles — the mathematics of gradient boosting, the statistics of honest evaluation, the code that implements both, and every number in the report. Read it top to bottom and you will be able to defend any line of the submission.

The one-sentence version

You predict which credit-card clients will default next month, using gradient-boosted decision trees; you prove the prediction is better than simpler alternatives, that the improvement is not an artefact of leakage or luck, that the resulting probabilities are calibrated enough to make a cost-optimal accept/reject decision, and that the model's reasoning can be audited feature-by-feature.

1.1 The deliverables and where the marks are

The brief splits 100 marks across five sections. The split is lopsided, and the whole shape of the report follows from it.

SectionMarksWhat it actually has to contain
Introduction10Problem framing, reused from the Phase 1 proposal
Solution50Investigation of the tool (how xgboost works, core functions, real code) plus justification of every design choice
Key results & metrics20Tables and charts, stated tersely
Summary15What worked, what didn't, what surprised you
References5APA 7th, consistent
Why this matters for how you talk about the work

Half the marks are for reasoning, not results. A perfect ROC-AUC with no justification scores badly; a modest ROC-AUC with a watertight argument for every choice scores well. That is why the report moves explanation up into Solution and leaves Key Results as bare tables — and why this guide spends far more time on why than on what.

1.2 Two budgets that do not interact

The page cap (10 pages, 10% penalty per page over) constrains what is printed, not what is run. So the project deliberately runs wide and prints narrow:

  • 14 experiments were run and committed to results/*.json.
  • The body carries 5 tables and 2 figures; everything else lives in the uncapped appendix.
  • The governing rule: never cut an experiment for page reasons; cut its presentation.

The measured body came in at 9 of 10 pages, confirmed by Word itself rather than by a character-count proxy.

1.3 The two research questions everything serves

Phase 1 committed to two questions, verbatim. Every experiment exists to answer one of them.

Q1
"Does the marginal performance gain achieved by ensemble architectures and non-linear models justify the corresponding loss in direct rule-based interpretability?" Answered by the complexity ladder (§6) — eight rungs from a majority-class constant to a tuned ensemble, each adding capability at a measurable interpretability cost. Not answered by a single XGBoost number.
Q2
"Can post-hoc explainability techniques bridge this gap by providing regulatory-compliant decision auditability for non-linear ensemble models?" Answered by the SHAP sections (§11–§12) — including the stability check, because an explanation that reorders under reseeding is not an audit trail.

Two architectural rules that make the numbers reconcile
  1. All evaluation goes through runner.cv_evaluate(). Divergent CV loops are the single biggest source of numbers that cannot be reconciled later.
  2. Every number printed in the report is read back out of results/, never retyped. src/report.py builds each table from the JSON. This guide obeys the same rule — its data is injected at build time by guide/build_guide.py.
