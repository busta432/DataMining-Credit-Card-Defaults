Section 12Is the explanation fit for audit?

§11 produced an explanation with a uniqueness theorem behind it. That theorem is conditional on the fitted model, which leaves the question an auditor actually cares about unanswered: would you get the same explanation if you re-ran the pipeline? And would you get the same answer if you had used a different importance measure? Two experiments, and the second one returns a result that should make anyone nervous about feature_importances_.

12.1 Stability under reseeding — the audit-trail test

Refit the final configuration with random_state ∈ {}, recompute SHAP for each fit, and compare the resulting global rankings. Nothing else changes: same data, same splits, same hyperparameters, same explainer configuration. Only the integer moves.

Why the seed moves anything at all

If boosting were deterministic the answer would be trivially "the same". It is not, because two tuned parameters inject randomness on every tree: subsample = draws a different row sample per tree, and colsample_bytree = draws a different column subset. So each seed explores a genuinely different sequence of trees, and when two features carry overlapping information the choice of which one a given tree splits on is decided partly by which was offered. The seed is not a formality here; it is the mechanism by which collinearity turns into attribution noise.

Two measures, because they fail differently

Reporting both is deliberate. Spearman alone can look poor because of churn in the bottom ten features, which is irrelevant to an audit. Jaccard alone can look perfect while the top two swap, which is very relevant. Together they bracket the question.

12.2 Where the instability is — and it is exactly where §11 predicted

The aggregate numbers are good, but an average can hide a concentrated problem. Decomposing rank SD by feature block gives the diagnostic:

This is a prediction being confirmed, not a problem being discovered

§11.9 stated the caveat a priori, reasoning from the Phase 1 VIF: when features carry near-identical information, TreeSHAP's split of credit among them is partly arbitrary. §12 measures the arbitrariness and finds it 49% larger inside the collinear block than outside it.

That chain — Phase 1 measures VIF → §11 predicts attribution instability → §12 measures it → the fix is to read the block as a group — is a complete analytical loop across both phases of the project. It is also the correct response to collinearity for a tree model: the columns are kept, because §10's ablations show prediction is unharmed, and the reporting is adjusted instead. Dropping features to make an explanation tidier would be sacrificing the model to flatter the audit.

12.3 Five importance measures that do not agree

Everything above concerns SHAP's reproducibility. This subsection asks a different and more uncomfortable question: does the choice of importance measure change the answer? XGBoost ships four built-in measures, sklearn offers permutation importance, and exp12 computes all five plus SHAP on the same fitted model.

By weight, BILL_AMT1 is the most important feature in the model and PAY_0 is mid-table. By every held-out measure, PAY_0 dominates. The mechanism is not mysterious: a continuous balance in New Taiwan dollars offers the hist binner (§3.4) hundreds of usable thresholds and gets revisited deep in every tree for small refinements, while PAY_0 has eight possible cut points and spends them near the root where they are worth the most. Counting splits measures opportunity, not contribution.

Why showing the disagreement is worth more than picking a winner quietly

Most submissions call model.feature_importances_, print the bar chart, and move on. That attribute returns gain by default in XGBClassifier — a training-loss quantity — and almost nobody says which measure they used, because almost nobody knows there is a choice.

Demonstrating that cover and weight anti-correlate at on this very model proves the choice is load-bearing, and that makes the justification below a real argument rather than a formality. It also connects directly to §11.4: there the cross-check showed the perturbation choice was not load-bearing (ρ = ), so it was reported in one line and set aside. Knowing which of your design decisions matter is the skill being demonstrated in both places.

The argument for which to trust

1
Held-out beats in-sample. gain, total_gain, cover and weight are all read off the fitted trees — training-set artefacts. SHAP and permutation importance are evaluated on rows the model never saw. The entire §5 protocol exists because in-sample quantities mislead; there is no reason to abandon that principle for importance.
2
Predictions beat structure. "How often was this column split on" is a fact about the model's shape. "How much does this column move the output" is a fact about its behaviour. Only the second is what anyone means by importance.
3
Local beats global-only. SHAP decomposes an individual decision; permutation importance cannot. Since the deliverable is decision auditability, that breaks the tie in SHAP's favour — and it is why SHAP is the measure reported in the body and the other five live in the appendix.
4
They agree anyway, which is the reassuring part. SHAP and permutation correlate at , and SHAP and gain at . The two independent held-out measures broadly concur, and they both concur with the strongest in-sample measure. The outliers are weight and cover, i.e. the two that count structure rather than effect.

Two details that are easy to get wrong
  • booster.get_score() returns a dict keyed only by features that were actually split on. Converting it to an array with a plain list comprehension raises KeyError for any unused feature, and — worse — using dict.get(f) without a default silently yields None, which propagates into the Spearman computation as NaN and quietly poisons a whole row of the agreement matrix. The explicit 0.0 default is correct: never split on means zero importance, not missing importance.
  • permutation_importance is given X_val, not X_train. Permuting a column and measuring the training-score drop reports how much the model memorised through that column. That would convert the one unambiguously held-out built-in alternative into another in-sample measure and destroy the point of including it.

12.5 SHAP interaction values

Equation (24) attributes to single features. Shapley interaction values extend it to pairs, splitting each φj into a main effect plus pairwise terms, so that a feature's influence can be separated from its influence in combination with another:

f(x) = φ0 + Σj φjmain + Σj<k φjk(28)

The cost is a factor of M: the output is 23 × 23 per client rather than 23, and TreeSHAP's complexity gains a feature-count multiplier. exp12 therefore restricts interactions to the top five features — the plan listed this as runtime-permitting and it ran in overall.

The interaction that matters, and how to describe it carefully

The substantive pair is PAY_0 × PAY_2, which is exactly what §11.7's dependence plot is coloured by. The reading: a single month of delinquency is bad; a consecutive run is worse than the sum of its parts, because two months at code 2 is evidence of a persistent inability to pay rather than one missed cheque. An additive model cannot express that — it is precisely the non-additivity §6.5 measured as the gap between the logistic rung and the tree rung.

The caution: interaction values inherit every caveat of §11.9 and amplify the collinearity one, because pairs drawn from inside the BILL_AMT block are credit-splitting artefacts rather than findings. They are reported for the PAY_n block, where the columns are distinct ordinal codes with distinct meanings, and the monetary pairs are not interpreted individually. This is why the interaction result sits in the appendix with one sentence in the body rather than being promoted to a headline.

12.6 The answer to research question 2

Stated with its scope attached

Yes, with three documented limits. Post-hoc explanation does bridge the interpretability gap §6 measured, to the standard "explain why this decision was made, reproducibly, for this individual":

  • It is exact. Attributions sum to the model output to , so no part of a decision is unexplained (§11.3).
  • It is unique. It is the only attribution satisfying local accuracy, missingness, consistency and symmetry — so "why SHAP" has an answer that is not an appeal to convention (§11.2).
  • It is reproducible. Spearman , top-10 Jaccard across five reseeded fits, with PAY_0 rank 1 every time (§12.1).
  • It is externally corroborated. The attributions independently reproduce a non-monotonic gradient measured in Phase 1 from the raw data, at ρ = (§11.7).

The three limits, each of which must be stated rather than buried: individual BILL_AMT attributions are unstable and must be read as a block (§12.2); the explanation is of the model, not of a causal mechanism (§11.9); and the choice of importance measure materially changes the global story, so it must be declared — this project declares SHAP, and §12.3 is the argument for it.

Self-check: five seeds is a small sample. Would ten have been better?

Marginally, and the cost is linear — each seed is a full refit plus a full SHAP computation. The reason five suffices is what the result has to establish: the claim is not "rank SD equals 1.73 ± ε", it is "the top of the ranking does not churn". Five fits give ten pairwise comparisons, and all ten agree — PAY_0 first and LIMIT_BAL second in every single fit, with the worst top-10 Jaccard at , i.e. nine of ten features shared. A sixth through tenth seed would tighten the estimate of a quantity nobody needs estimated tightly.

Note also that §14 runs ten seeds for the performance spread, where the number is the deliverable and an SD estimated from five would be uncomfortably noisy. The sample sizes differ because the claims differ, and that is the right way round.

Self-check: if weight is so misleading, why did XGBoost make it available at all?

Because it answers a legitimate question — just not the one people use it for. weight is a diagnostic about the model's structure: a feature split on thousands of times at depth 7 is being used for fine-grained refinement, which tells you something real about how the ensemble is spending its capacity, and is genuinely useful when debugging overfitting or deciding what max_depth is doing.

The failure is one of defaults and documentation, not of the measure. It became "feature importance" by being cheap to compute and present in the API, and because plot_importance() uses it as its default even though feature_importances_ uses gain — so the same library can hand you two different rankings depending on which function you call. Being able to explain that discrepancy, with the measured anti-correlation of to back it up, is a much stronger position than having never noticed it.

Self-check: could an auditor object that you chose the importance measure that supported your story?

They could, and the defence has to be structural rather than rhetorical. Three things make it hold.

The choice was argued on method, not outcome. The criteria — held-out rather than in-sample, prediction-based rather than structure-based, locally decomposable rather than global-only — are stated in §12.3 and would have selected SHAP before any number was computed. They are not post-hoc rationalisations of a preferred ranking.

All five competing measures are reported. The full agreement matrix and per-feature values are in results/exp12_stability.json and the appendix, including the one measure (weight) that contradicts the headline. A cherry-pick would have omitted it.

The conclusion is robust to the choice anyway. PAY_0 ranks first under SHAP, gain, total_gain and permutation importance — four of six measures, including both held-out ones. Only the two pure structure-counters disagree, and §12.3 explains mechanically why they would. The story does not depend on the choice; the precision of the story does.
